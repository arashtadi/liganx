## Honest read of each result

**EGFR T790M / gefitinib (✓ both):** clean win. Vina says T790M weakens gefitinib by ~0.7 kcal/mol; literature says ~0.9 kcal/mol. This is the reference T790M-resistance result everyone cites, and Liganx reproduces it.

**EGFR T790M / osimertinib (✗ sign):** **WRONG direction**. Real biology: osimertinib was *designed* to bind T790M tighter than WT — that's the entire point of the third-generation TKI series, and the published Kd shifts from ~12 nM (L858R) to ~1 nM (L858R/T790M). Vina says the opposite. This is a known hard case for physics-only docking: T790M's effect is partly conformational (alters the gatekeeper's hydrophobic packing in a way that *favors* osimertinib's covalent geometry), which a rigid-receptor Vina dock cannot capture. A CNN re-rank (which is currently disabled on our Blackwell pod) might catch it.

**KRAS G12C / sotorasib (✓ sign, ✗ magnitude):** Vina gets the direction right (+0.30 kcal/mol = "WT slightly weaker than mutant") but underestimates the magnitude by ~13x (0.3 vs 4.1 kcal/mol expected). Reason is structural: sotorasib's 1000-fold selectivity comes from **forming a covalent bond to Cys12** in G12C — a bond Vina doesn't model at all. The 0.3 kcal/mol Vina shift is just the trivial steric difference between Gly and Cys. The biology happens after the bond forms, and Vina sees only the pre-bond pose. This is an inherent limitation of non-covalent docking, not a Liganx bug.

**KRAS G12C / adagrasib (✓ sign, ✗ magnitude):** same story as sotorasib. Adagrasib is also a covalent G12C inhibitor; Vina captures only the pre-binding noncovalent pose. Sign right, magnitude off by ~5x.

**BRAF V600E / vemurafenib (failed):** QuickVina-GPU returned rc=255 for vemurafenib on both WT and V600E BRAF. Same misleading "compound too large or flexible" error. Vemurafenib is MW 489.92 with ~8 rotatable bonds (propylsulfonyl + halogenated aryl + azaindole) — likely hitting the QuickVina2-GPU `--max_rot` kernel cap. The rc=127 (libboost) and rc=255 (OpenCL ICD) issues from earlier today are fixed, so this is a *third* rc=255 cause specific to ligand flexibility. Worth investigating.

## What this tells us

**The product correctly predicts the *direction* of mutation effect 75% of the time on this small sample.** That's a meaningful number — better than coin flip, in line with what's published for Vina-based mutation-aware docking on similar test sets. Sign correctness is the most important property for the headline use case ("does this mutation make this drug worse?") and we get it right for the EGFR/KRAS cases that match the typical user query.

**The product underestimates magnitude on covalent inhibitors** (sotorasib, adagrasib) because Vina is a non-covalent scoring function. For G12C work, users should be told: "Vina captures non-covalent binding only; the headline G12C selectivity comes from covalent bond formation that this score doesn't include." This should probably be in the UI as a target-specific note.

**The product gets osimertinib wrong** — and this is the most important miss. Osimertinib + T790M is the textbook example of the mutation/inhibitor relationship that Liganx is built to characterize. The CNN re-rank from GNINA was the planned mitigation, but is currently disabled (Blackwell incompatibility). Real fix is the GNINA from-source rebuild for sm_120 we deferred earlier.

**One in five cases hard-failed at the pod level**, with a misleading user-facing error. Vemurafenib is a real-world clinically-used compound; "compound too large" is wrong (MW 490) and unhelpful. We should verify whether QuickVina2-GPU has a hardcoded rotatable-bond cap, distinguish that from the other rc=255 modes, and either route around it (CPU Vina fallback for high-flex compounds) or surface a more accurate message.

## What's NOT in this benchmark and matters

- **Pose validation.** PoseBusters / pocket-drift were not graded here. A "correct ΔΔG by accident" with a non-physical pose is still wrong. Future versions of this benchmark should also score pose physical sanity.
- **Repeatability.** Vina is stochastic at exhaustiveness=4. We ran each case once. To know whether a number is real or noise, each case should be repeated 5x and the std reported. Adagrasib's +0.60 may be ±0.7 across re-runs.
- **The 5 unreached targets** (ABL, KIT, ALK ×2, JAK2). Adding them to the catalog would let us reach the ≥8/10 sign-agreement bar from the README's grading rubric. Right now we have 3/4 = 75%, just under the 80% bar.

## Recommended next actions

1. **Add a UI note for covalent inhibitors.** Target-specific sentence on G12C and other Cys-targeting cases: "Vina cannot model covalent bond formation. The 1000-fold G12C selectivity reported in the literature is post-bond. Use this score as a non-covalent fit estimate only."
2. **Investigate vemurafenib failure.** Check QuickVina2-GPU `--max_rot` cap (likely 32 or 16 in the kernel). Either route highly flexible compounds to CPU Vina or surface a clearer error.
3. **Schedule rebuild of GNINA for sm_120.** The osimertinib miss is exactly the kind of conformational subtlety where a CNN re-rank helps. Estimated 30–60 min compile time on the Blackwell pod.
4. **Extend the catalog** to ABL, KIT, ALK, JAK2 so the remaining 5 cases can run. Then re-run the full benchmark and report 10/10 numbers.
5. **Add std-dev tracking.** Each case ×5 runs, report mean ± std. Without this, we can't tell signal from noise.
